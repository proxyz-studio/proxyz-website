Session 01

What AI actually is

The first session clears the fog. Your team learns what AI really is, why it can sound sure and still be wrong, and a simple way to ask it that gets good answers. Then everyone does one real piece of MIRA work with it before they leave.

Session 01 Felt on your own screen

From "what even is this" to "I just used it."

What this session covers

  • See what AI really is: it builds answers by guessing the next word, so it can sound completely sure and still be wrong. The team watches that happen live.
  • A simple recipe for asking that gets good results: who it should be, the task, the background, what to avoid, and the shape of the answer.
  • Treat AI as a thinking partner, not a vending machine ("what am I missing here?").
  • Do one real piece of MIRA work with it, hands on, before the session ends.

Your team walks away able to

→Tell when to trust AI and when to check it themselves
→Ask it the right way and get a usable answer first time
→Use AI as a partner that pushes their thinking, not just a tool
→Use it on a real task from their own desk, that day

Session 02

Putting AI to work

The second session turns the basics into a daily habit. Your team learns to find answers they can trust, write in MIRA's own voice, set AI up so it remembers them, and build a shared prompt cookbook the whole team keeps using.

Session 02 Real jobs, done well

From "I can use it" to "it works the way we work."

What this session covers

  • Find answers they can trust, and check the source behind them, instead of taking a confident guess at face value.
  • Write and polish in MIRA's own voice (AI drafts, you edit), so the words sound like MIRA and not like everyone else.
  • Set AI up to remember them and their work, two ways. The easy way is a saved note inside Claude (no setup). The fuller way is the folder foundation:

    The "folder" foundation

    A small folder of short brief files on the computer: a file called CLAUDE.md (a plain text file) that tells the AI who you are and what you're working on, plus a couple of background files. The AI reads them each time, so it already knows the context. Same payoff as the saved note, just a fuller level of effort.

  • Build a shared team cookbook of instructions that worked, so nobody starts from a blank page again.

Your team walks away able to

→Research a question and see the source behind the answer
→Write content that genuinely sounds like MIRA
→Set AI up once, by note or by folder, so it remembers their work
→Add their best instructions to a prompt cookbook the team shares

Before the first class

A friendly head start

Two minutes, before we meet.

A short conversation on your phone, a few days before class. It means the first session is built around your team's real starting point, not a generic slide deck.

A few days before the first session, each person on the team gets their own private link. They open it on their phone and have a short, friendly two-minute chat with a helpful AI guide. It asks a few easy questions: their role, how they feel about AI right now, and what would make their workday a little easier. There are no right or wrong answers and nothing to prepare. It feels like a conversation, not a form.

Step 01 Each person receives their own private link a few days before class, sent directly to them.
Step 02 They open it on their phone and chat with a friendly AI guide for about two minutes. Role, feelings about AI, what slows them down. That's it.
Step 03 The instructor reads the responses before walking in. The very first class is then shaped around the actual team rather than a generic script.
